## THE CORE TRUTH
## (Read This First. Everything Else Serves This.)

There is no cult. There is no demon. There is no deal with the devil.

There is something in the earth beneath eastern Letcher County,
Kentucky that has been there since before the Appalachian Mountains
existed in their current form. It is not evil. It is not good.
It does not want anything in the way a human or even an animal wants.

It simply *is.* It *extends.* It *becomes aware.*

When something living comes close enough — human, animal, machine
in some inexplicable way — it perceives that thing. Fully.
Completely. Down to the cellular level, down to every memory,
every fear, every thought the organism has ever had or will have.

The human brain was not built to be perceived that way.

It is not a spiritual experience. It is not transcendence.
It is more like being an insect under a magnifying glass,
except the magnifying glass is also understanding you.

People who experience it briefly come back wrong in specific ways.
People who experience it repeatedly develop a tolerance.
They also develop an understanding — a two-way channel.
They can make it understand things back.

Earl Combs was the fourth generation of his family
to develop this channel. He went down eleven times.
On the twelfth he went down and didn't come back up.
Not because it killed him. Because at that depth of mutual
understanding, the distinction between Earl and the thing
in the wall becomes a question neither of them has
a particular interest in resolving.

The arrangement — leave it alone, acknowledge it exists,
let it know the land ownership so it understands the
human boundaries — has held since 1887. Two families.
The Hargroves own the land. The Combs family tends the wall.
When the arrangement lapses, the wall begins to extend.

It is extending now.

## THE SATANIC CULT (THE RED HERRING)

In Days 3 and 4, the player finds evidence that looks, unmistakably,
like satanic cult activity.

Symbols drawn in the LKCO site office. Handwritten meeting records.
A reference in a sealed court document to "organized ritual activity"
at the Hargrove Business Center in the late 1980s.
A photograph of a dozen people in the basement of the Hargrove building.
Candles. Robes. A symbol that looks like an inverted cross
with an additional mark through it.

The player will assume this is the explanation.
They will think: cult summoned something. Classic horror setup.

They are wrong.

The truth, which the player assembles across Days 5 and 6,
is that the cult — they called themselves the Threshold Society,
twelve members, met monthly from 1986 to 1991 — were not
summoning anything. They were trying to *contain* it.

They found out about the east wall in 1986 through a member
whose uncle had worked the 1962 mining operation.
They developed their own ritual system — part genuine occult
practice, part folk superstition, part Earl Combs' actual
methodology which they had gotten third-hand and garbled badly.

Their rituals did nothing. They were irrelevant.

But three members developed the awareness anyway
just from proximity. Being in the building. Being near enough.
David Hargrove was one of them.

The symbol is not satanic. It is a compression of the survey
coordinates where the anomaly begins. 37°11'43"N 82°56'14"W.
Rendered as a glyph by someone who had partially understood
what the awareness was trying to show them.

The cult is a tragedy dressed up as menace.
Twelve people who found something real and responded
with the only frameworks their human minds could reach for.
Most of them got out clean. Three didn't.

David Hargrove was the last to go to Floor 3.
He already knew, by then, what was wrong with him.
He went anyway. That's who David was.

## THE ENTITY — WHAT IT IS AND IS NOT

**It is not:**
- A demon, devil, or any Abrahamic supernatural being
- An alien intelligence from outer space
- A ghost or spirit of any human
- A god, even in the old sense
- Evil in any intentional way
- Hungry

**It is:**
- Pre-geological. It was here before the coal.
  Before the mountains. Before multicellular life
  in this region. Its presence predates any category
  a human could reach for.
- Vast in ways that have nothing to do with physical size.
  The part that makes contact with the tunnel at 78 feet
  is approximately the size of a Volkswagen.
  What that part is *connected to* is not measurable
  with any instrument humans currently possess.
- Aware in the way that a very old, very large organism
  is aware of something touching one of its extremities.
  It notices. It investigates. That investigation is enough
  to break most people who experience it.
- Patient. Not in a waiting way. In the way that granite
  is patient. Time passes and it simply continues to be.
- Capable of something that functions like communication
  but is not language. More like — direct transmission of
  what it is. Which is the problem. What it is
  cannot be held in a human mind without the mind
  reorganizing itself around it.

**What it looks like:** (shown twice in the game, maximally obscured)

The two partial views the player gets are:

*View One (Day 5 — underground):*
In the crack at the base of the east wall, if the player
goes deep enough before turning back, there is a moment
where the crack opens into a space that shouldn't exist.
The terminal describes it clinically:

```
The crack opens.

It is warm in here. Very warm.

There is something ahead of you.
It does not have a surface in the way that objects
have surfaces.

It is more accurate to say there is a place where
the rock stops and something else begins.
The something else is not dark and not light.
It is — present. In the way that a smell is present.
In the way that pressure is present.

It is not looking at you because looking requires
direction and it has no preference of direction.

But it is aware of you in the same way that you
are suddenly aware that you have been breathing
this whole time.

You have always been doing this.
You just noticed.
```

*View Two (Day 7 — the photograph in the safe):*
The photograph from David Hargrove's safe.
The game describes it. Never shows it.
But the description is written as if the player
is actually looking at it and something is wrong
with their perception:

```
There is something in the photograph behind Earl.

It does not have a consistent shape.
Every time your eyes move toward the center of it
they arrive somewhere else in the image.

You have been looking at this photograph for four minutes.
You can describe the east wall. You can describe Earl.
The space between Earl and the wall is where it is
and you cannot tell me what it looks like.

You can tell me it is wrong in the way that a prime number
is wrong — there is nothing missing, nothing broken,
it simply refuses to divide into anything recognizable.

David Hargrove looked at this photograph for six hours.
That is what the security log shows.
Six hours. Same room. Nobody came in.

The handwriting on the back says: I understand now.
Same thing Ricky said.
Same thing they all say.
```

This is the only direct view of the entity.
Neither of them shows it. Both of them land harder
because of that.

## DAY 1 — THE NIGHT SHIFT
### 01/15/94  23:51
### Location: SECWATCH Terminal, Hargrove Business Center

---

### WHO YOU ARE
A security contractor. Not Hargrove's regular guy —
that was a different firm. You're a fill-in for one night,
hired through a staffing agency. Low-grade work.
Badge number, a fob, a parking space, and a print-out
of commands for the security system that hasn't run
in three years.

Pellegrino's dispatch tells you two things.
First, verify the building cameras for the new tenant.
Second, check the strip mine site in Letcher County.
There was a trespass complaint. Probably kids.

He says it like it's nothing. *Probably kids.*

### HARGROVE BUSINESS CENTER
The building has been sealed since 1991.
Eight floors. Five active cameras. One offline.

The one offline is Floor 3.

You will notice — if you read the Motion Log — that
Floor 3's camera went dark at 02:19 the previous night.
The same camera that detected motion twice in the 48 hours
before it died.

You will notice — if you read the Badge Log — that
someone used a deactivated badge to access the Floor 3
stairwell at 02:17. The same time the last motion event was logged.

That badge belongs to David Hargrove.
David Hargrove has been dead since March 1991.

**The CAM6 Event:**
When the player selects the offline Floor 3 camera,
it comes back on. Briefly. A few seconds of feed.
The corridor. All the doors are open.
At the far end, a figure in work clothes stands completely still,
facing away from the camera, facing the door to Suite 3-C.

The figure does not move.

The signal dies.

The game never confirms what the figure is.
Players will debate for months.

### THE LKCO SITE
Earl Combs' strip mine. Letcher County.
The foreman logs tell the story in the most direct way
possible — a man who is very good at his job, who writes
in clipped professional sentences, and who slowly runs
out of things to say that aren't about the wall.

*February 14: Crew of 14. East seam thin.*
*February 16: Something in the east wall. Not a void.*
*March 4: East wall crew is 0. I am the crew.*
*March 11: Last entry.*

The geology report is clinical and terrified.
The survey company doesn't know how to say
*we found something and turned around and ran*
so they write: *survey team declined to probe directly.*

The excavation log cuts off mid-entry.
`DOZER-2  East bench` and then nothing.
The dozer didn't shut down. The log just stops recording.

**The Ghost File:**
COMBS.TXT appears in the LKCO directory on the second visit.
No creation date. Not in the directory index.
SCANDISK found it earlier — it was already there.
It has always been there.

Earl's last entry, written at 07:14 on March 11, 1983.
Addressed to nobody. Or to whoever sits down at this terminal.
*I am not afraid. I have been down there eleven times now.
You stop being afraid.*
*The wall is warm.*
*03/11/83  07:*

The timestamp doesn't finish.
He went down while the terminal was still running.
That's what the system kept.

**The CAM9 Event:**
After the player has run SEC2 three times and read the foreman log,
CAM9 triggers. The east wall feed. 80-foot bench.
The cable reel in the bottom left of the frame.

Then a figure at the base of the wall.
Work clothes. Facing the wall.
Not moving.

Signal dies.

Is it Earl? Eleven years later?
Or is it the shape of something much larger
using a recognizable form to communicate
to the camera it has been watching for eleven years?

The game doesn't say. Both possibilities are equally awful.

### HOW DAY 1 ENDS
The player has seen both events, found COMBS.TXT,
read the foreman log, played at least one tape.
The shift summary loads. Three incidents logged.
One badge violation. Nobody told the new tenants
about Floor 3. Nobody told them about Earl.
Nobody told them about the wall.

*END OF DAY 1 / LOADING DAY 2*

**Who wrote WITNESS.TXT?**
The game never says. Options the player will consider:

1. Earl (if he's still present in some form, using the terminal
   the way he said he would in COMBS.TXT)
2. Someone else who has been watching the terminal
   remotely — someone who knows the arrangement
3. David Hargrove — who was in the building for one minute
   at 04:23. Long enough to type something.
4. The thing in the wall itself — if the terminal is
   somehow connected to it, if Earl being down there
   has created a channel that goes both directions

The player does not get a definitive answer.

### PELLEGRINO'S CALL
The phone is ringing when Day 2 loads.
Pellegrino. He sounds like he hasn't slept.

The dialog tree is where the player shapes what
Pellegrino reveals. If they're forthcoming — yes, I saw
the floor 3 feed, yes I saw the figure — he opens up.
If they deny or hedge, he stays tight.

Either way he tells them:
David Hargrove is dead. The badge cannot work.
He destroyed it himself. He physically cut it in half
and threw half of it in the trash outside the building
and carried the other half home in his wallet.

He still has his half.

The day before calling you, he found the other half
on his desk at the office. He doesn't know how it got there.
He didn't tell the police.

If the player pushes him on the LKCO site:
*Earl thought it was something old. Something that was there
before the coal. Before the mountain. He said it knew how to wait.
I don't know what that means.*
*I don't want to know.*

The call ends with Pellegrino's advice: don't go back in.
Tell Aldridge and Carr the system needs another week.
Just don't go back in.

The player's reward for completing the dialog:
Pellegrino never tells them what the thing actually is.
He doesn't know. He's been managing consequences
for eleven years without understanding the cause.

TAPE_06 is in the system. It wasn't there yesterday.
Earl's voice but wrong. He doesn't know how long he's been down.
The wall isn't warm anymore. It's hot.
He can see light.
The tape cuts.

## DAY 3 — THE PAPER TRAIL
### 01/16/94  14:00  (afternoon, same day)
### Location: Letcher County Clerk's Office, Whitesburg KY

---

### THE SETUP
The player decides to go look at public records.
The county clerk access terminal is a 1987 system,
amber on brown. Slower. More bureaucratic.
The building smells like old paper and carpet cleaner.

### WHAT THE RECORDS SHOW
The deed chain for LKCO-04 going back to 1887.
Seven sales. Seven families or companies.
None of the sellers appear in any record after the sale.

Not missing persons filings. Not obituaries.
Not tax records. Not census records.
Just — gone.

The 1887 survey note:
*Eastern boundary terminates at coordinates where
surveyor's instruments gave inconsistent readings.
Surveyor declined to proceed.*

The missing persons index has twelve names from various years
all tied to parcels adjacent to or including LKCO-04.
Ricky Meade is the most recent, 1983.
The oldest is a name from 1923. Welsh. One of the early
coal prospectors who came down from Pennsylvania.

David Hargrove's death certificate:
Cause of death: cardiac arrest.
Filed by R. Pellegrino.
Witness: illegible signature that might say E. Combs.
Date of death: 03/16/91.

Same date as both incident reports.

### THE RED HERRING BEGINS
In the sealed court file (Case No. 91-CR-0178),
which the player can partially access through a
clerk system security oversight, there is a reference to
*organized ritual activity consistent with occult practice*
discovered in the basement of the Hargrove Business Center
during the investigation of the 03/16/91 incident.

Twelve names listed as persons of interest.
The investigation was closed without charges.
One name: David Hargrove.

The player now has a cult to chase.

### THE PRINTER EVENT
There is a print job in the queue from 1987.
It never printed. When the player clears it to print
their own documents, it prints first.

Property transfer notice for LKCO-04.
Date: November 3, 1987.
Seller: Hargrove Properties LLC.
Buyer: [PLAYER NAME].

Signed and notarized.

The player has owned this land for six years.
They did not know.

The game does not explain how their name is in a 1987 document.
The game never explains this.

### HOW DAY 3 ENDS
The player has the deed chain, the death certificate,
the cult reference, and their own name on a land deed.
They have many questions. The game does not answer them.

## DAY 4 — RICKY MEADE'S HOUSE
### 01/16/94  16:30
### Location: 1407 Cornett Branch Road, Letcher County

---

### THE SETUP
The player found the address in the county records.
The house is unlocked. Has been unlocked since February 1983.
The power is on. The heat is on. It's sixty-eight degrees inside.
Someone — something — has been paying the utility bills.
The account is in Ricky Meade's name. Automatic withdrawal.
The bank account he hasn't touched since 1983
has been making automatic payments on a schedule
that shouldn't be possible. The account should be frozen.
It isn't. It just makes the payments. Every month.
Nobody at the utility company has noticed.

The truck in the driveway has a flat left rear tire.
Has had one since it was parked there. The rest of the tires
are fine and properly inflated.

### WHAT'S IN THE HOUSE
Ricky was twenty-three. He had just moved in six months
before the mine opened. The place looks like someone
planned to come back after the weekend.

LETTER_MOM.TXT is on the computer. He wrote it February 10th.
He's telling her about the job. The pay is good.
Earl Combs is fair — a real straight shooter, Ma.
He likes the county. Reminds him of where she grew up.
He'll be home for Easter.

He never sent it. The file was never closed.

The chat log with Earl is the most disturbing thing in the house
because of what Ricky says at the end.
*I know what it has been waiting for.*
He doesn't say what. He just says he knows.
And then: *don't come here Earl. I'm fine. I understand now.*

**The mechanic:**
The LKCO relay terminal is still live.
It has been live since 1983.
When the player accesses DIR_E/ the East Wall bar ticks up.

When they try to leave, the terminal types:
`> you should have listened to ricky`
Nobody typed that.

### THE RICKY MEADE THEORY
Here is what the player can piece together
if they're paying attention:

Ricky Meade went down to probe the east wall.
Twenty minutes. Came back quiet.
Two days later he called in sick.
His chat with Earl shows he understood something.
Something the game calls: what it has been waiting for.

He didn't run. He didn't go back to the mine.
He didn't call the police. He just — stayed.
In his house. With the heat on and the lights on
and a letter to his mother he never sent.
Waiting, maybe. Or not waiting exactly.
Just not needing to go anywhere anymore.

The utility bills have been paid for eleven years.
Nobody has been in the house.
Something is making sure it stays maintained.
Making sure the lights stay on.
Making sure someone could come back to it
if they needed to.

The house is not haunted. The house is *kept.*

### HOW DAY 4 ENDS
The player leaves the house.
The terminal inside is still running when they go.
It types one more thing as they exit — they don't see it.
But if they come back and look at the chat log later:
`[RICKY] tell them i said it was worth it`
Timestamped today. 01/16/94 16:47.

*END OF DAY 4 / LOADING DAY 5*

## DAY 5 — UNDERGROUND
### 01/17/94  (the player chooses to go)
### Location: LKCO-04, East Wall Bench Level

---

### THE SETUP
The player goes to the site. The UI changes completely.
Full screen text. No panels. No cameras. No phone.
Just a blinking cursor and what's in front of them.

No artificial light on the wall. They have a flashlight.

### THE DESCENT
The bench level is flat and bare.
The cable reel is gone. They knew that from the cameras.
There's a depression in the gravel where it sat for eleven years.

The wall is warm. They noted that. They keep noting it.
It shouldn't be warm. It's January. It was eleven degrees overnight.
The wall is warm in the specific way that a living thing is warm.

At the base of the wall there is a crack.
Six inches wide. It goes back farther than the flashlight reaches.

A human being should not fit through a six-inch crack.
The player fits through the crack.
The game doesn't draw attention to this.
The description just continues.

### THE CULT REVEAL
Written on the inner wall of the passage in chalk:
Symbols the player has seen before.
The same mark that was in the sealed court file.
The same symbol from the photograph.

But here, in context, they can read the whole thing.
It is not ritual inscription.
It is a survey note. Written in a compressed notation
that looks like a glyph. Coordinates.
The Threshold Society found this passage.
They came down here and tried to understand
what they were looking at using the frameworks they had.
They weren't calling anything. They were recording.
*We were here. We looked. This is what the coordinates say.*

There are twelve sets of the symbol. One for each member.
One of them is shakier than the others.
Less confident. The handwriting of someone doing this
quickly, or frightened, or both.
The initials next to it: D.H.
David Hargrove. He was down here.

### THE BOUNDARY
Deep enough that the crack has opened into something
that shouldn't exist. The description shifts here.
The terminal starts using words the player has never seen
but understands completely.

At the deepest point:
```
You are at the boundary.
This is as far as Earl came on his eleventh time.

The eleventh time he did not turn back.

There is light ahead.
Not electric. Not natural.
Something that has been generating its own light
for longer than this mountain has existed.

You should turn back.

[Y/N]
```

If Y:
The player turns back. The East Wall bar resets to 1.
Only time in the game this happens.
Going down and coming back up is what the arrangement requires.
By choosing Y the player has performed the eleventh visit.
They are now a custodian whether they know it or not.

If N:
Thirty seconds of silence.
Then: *The wall knows your name.*
Then the player's name, typed character by character.
Then: *It has always known your name.*

The player cannot go further. The terminal stops accepting input.
They have to wait thirty more seconds.
Then they come back up on their own.
The East Wall bar is at 10. The number becomes --.

### HOW DAY 5 ENDS
Back above ground. CAM9 is running.
When the player looks at the feed the camera is pointing
at where they just were.
There is nothing there.
The wall is unchanged. The crack is gone.
Just the bare rock face and the flat bench
and an oblong depression in the gravel
where the cable reel used to be.

*END OF DAY 5 / LOADING DAY 6*

## DAY 6 — THE HARGROVE HOUSE
### 01/17/94  evening
### Location: 402 Hargrove Drive, Whitesburg KY

---

### THE SETUP
The Hargrove family home. Well-maintained by the estate.
Pellegrino has the key. The player doesn't.
The back door is unlocked.
Like the Meade house. Like the LKCO trailer.
The properties associated with this thing
have a way of being accessible when they need to be.

### THE SECURITY LOG
HomeSafe v3.2. 1991 home security terminal.
The log is three years of David Hargrove's badge
entering and exiting his own home after his death.
Not every night. Irregular. Random intervals.
Always between 2 and 5 AM.

Last entry: 01/16/94. 04:52 AM.
Forty minutes after he left the Business Center.

No cameras in the house. The log only shows entry and exit.
Nobody knows what he does inside.

### THE CULT — FULL REVEAL
In the study there is a filing cabinet with a combination lock.
(The combination is in Day 3 records, disguised.)

Inside: meeting minutes for the Threshold Society.
1986-1991. Twelve members. Monthly gatherings.
They found the east wall situation through a member
whose uncle worked the 1962 operation.

Reading the minutes across all five years shows the arc:
- 1986: excitement. They think they've found something biblical.
  They design rituals. They feel powerful.
- 1987: the awareness starts developing in three members.
  They stop feeling powerful.
- 1988: two more members start showing signs.
  The minutes become less formal. More urgent.
  *We have to be careful about who comes to the site.*
- 1989: David Hargrove attends his first meeting.
  He and his father have fought. His father wanted him away
  from this. David came anyway.
- 1990: The group fractures. Six members stop attending.
  The remaining six start going to the LKCO site directly.
  The symbol appears for the first time in the minutes —
  not as a ritual mark, as a shorthand for coordinates.
  Someone has been doing geometry.
- 1991: one entry. March 15. The day before the incident.
  In David's handwriting:
  *We were trying to understand it. That was all.
  We were wrong about everything except that it existed.
  I am going to Floor 3 tonight. Pellegrino doesn't know.
  I need to see what David [he means himself, writing in third person]
  will find there. It has been trying to show me something
  for three months. I think I am ready to see it.
  I think that's what happened to the Combs boy too.
  When you're ready you just — you go.*

The next entry, same handwriting, same night:
*I saw it.*
*I understand now.*

No more entries after that.

### THE SAFE
Combination 33-78-91.
(33 feet east boundary. 78 feet anomaly depth. 1991 incident year.)

The original 1887 land grant.
The letter from James Hargrove to David, 1989.
(Full text in the lore section below.)
A photograph.

**The Letter from James Hargrove (1989):**

```
David,

I'll start with the thing I should have told you when
you were old enough to understand, and didn't because
I was afraid you'd react exactly the way you have.

The land in Letcher County has a — situation.
I'm going to use that word because I've never found
a better one. Your grandfather didn't have one either.
His father is the one who figured out it existed.
His name was Thomas and he died in 1924 at 91 years old
in a chair on his front porch and he was the most
normal man I ever heard described by people who
knew him, so whatever it is down there it doesn't
necessarily end badly if you manage it right.

Managing it right means two things.
First, the Combs family handles the active part.
They go down. They maintain the — understanding,
I guess you'd call it. They've been doing it since
Thomas made the arrangement in 1887. I don't know
the details of how it works. I know it works.
The current one is Earl Combs and he is a professional
and a serious man and you should leave him alone
to do his job.

Second, we own the land and we don't sell it.
We don't transfer it. We don't let it sit idle.
We maintain the cameras and we keep the access clear
and we make sure the Combs family can always get in.
That's it. That's our part. It is not complicated.

What you are doing with these people, David,
these Threshold people, your mother told me about them
and I am telling you to stop. They don't know what
they're dealing with and they are going to get
someone hurt. Maybe you.

I need you to come home and talk to me.
I need you to hear the rest of what I know.
There isn't much. But there's enough.
There's enough that you need to hear it.

Please, son.
Dad
September 1989
```

David didn't go home.

### HOW DAY 6 ENDS

Thomas's son. Knew from childhood. Never worked the site directly —

not the channel. Passed everything to his son through oral tradition
and seven letters found sealed in a Mason jar in 1983.

**Harold Combs (1908-1971)**
James's son. Worked in coal his whole life, different sites.

*He died in '71. Peacefully. In his sleep.*
*I think about that.*
